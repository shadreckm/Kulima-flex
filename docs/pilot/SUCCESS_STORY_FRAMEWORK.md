# Success Story Collection Framework

How a closed loop becomes something worth telling — without ever overselling the
platform. The credibility of Kulima FLEX *is* its honesty, so a success story that
over-claims damages the product it is meant to promote.

## What counts as a "success" here

Not a happy testimonial. A success is a **decision that moved through the evidence chain
and came back with a learning**. Five story types, in descending order of value:

| Type | What it proves | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 Caught the flaw** | the platform stopped a decision resting on weak or contradictory evidence | rated outcome + the contradiction/unsupported-claim it acted on |
| **S2 Closed the loop** | an institution can show a board/donor decision → action → outcome → lesson | Outcome Review PDF + all five lesson fields |
| **S3 Changed the decision** | the recommendation altered what they did (fully or partially followed) | `recommendation_followed` + comment naming the signal or memo |
| **S4 Made it defensible** | the same decision, less time and more traceable justification | H3/H4 evidence: timing log + confidence statement in their words |
| **S5 Speed (Tourism)** | a 48-hour decision kept its speed and still got recorded | one-tap capture under a minute + rated outcome in-season |

An **S1 story is the strongest proof of the Trust Layer**, and it is a *negative* result
for the documents, not the platform. Collect them deliberately.

## Evidence tiers — decide the tier before you write a word

| Tier | Definition | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Verified** | rated outcome + written lesson + Outcome Review PDF + organisation sign-off | named case study, board packs, proposals, public |
| **B — Partial** | follow-up answered, outcome not yet rated | internal pipeline only; may be described as "in progress", never as a result |
| **C — Engagement only** | report downloaded, no follow-up answer | **not publishable as a success story**; usable only as adoption data |
| **X — Demo/synthetic** | demo record, example text, or another organisation's result | **never** used; excluded from every count |

Tier is a property of the record, not of the writing. A beautifully written Tier B story
is still Tier B and cannot imply a result.

## The pipeline

```
R4 closure review → case study drafted (#7) → tier assigned → organisation approval
   → added to the story library → routed to an audience → re-checked every 90 days
```

1. **Draft only from a closed loop.** The case study (#7) is the raw material; the story
   is a one-page re-telling of it for one audience.
2. **Assign the tier out loud** and write it at the top of the draft.
3. **Approve the exact text** — not "approval in principle". Name, role, date,
   identifiable or anonymised, quotations permitted or paraphrased.
4. **Route by audience:** donor/partner (S1, S2), board (S2, S4), bank/investor (S5, S3),
   sector peer (S3, S4).
5. **Re-check every 90 days.** If an outcome gets revised (a "successful" programme
   later fails), the story is **updated or withdrawn**, not quietly kept.

## Sentence rules (these make or break trust)

**Never write:**

- "Kulima FLEX improved decision quality by X %" — no aggregate claim is available until
  ≥ 10 rated outcomes exist **in the same trust band**; below that the product itself
  prints `NOT MEASURED`, and so must the story.
- "AI gave the recommendation" / "the model decided" — the platform produced evidence and
  a band; a named human decided.
- Any number that is not in the record or supplied by the organisation with a source.
- Testimonials that praise accuracy the record does not show; "the verdict was right"
  needs the re-test-the-band sentence from R4.
- Implied comparison to a baseline nobody measured.

**Always write:**

- The institution's own words for outcome and lesson (verbatim where approved).
- The limits: "trust score on this document set was modest", "one contradiction was
  flagged and the team resolved it with a second source".
- The chain, explicitly: *information → evidence → trust → signals → decision → report →
  action → outcome → learning*, naming where this story sits on it.
- Honest emptiness where relevant: "no success rate is claimed — too few rated outcomes
  exist yet".

## Collection mechanics (what the mentor asks, and when)

Ask at R4, in the closure review, on the call:

1. *What would you have done without this assessment?* → the counterfactual, in their words.
2. *What did it stop you from doing?* → the S1 candidate.
3. *What did the platform get wrong or miss?* → mandatory; a story without this line is
   not approved. It is also the pilot's most valuable product finding.
4. *Who else in your sector faces this exact decision?* → next cohort recruitment.
5. *Would you let us tell this, with your name, in your words?* → consent, logged.

## Story library (maintained as a simple list — no tooling needed)

| ID | Organisation (or label) | Profile | Story type | Tier | Decision date | Outcome status | Approved | Audience | Last re-check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Minimum pilot output: **3 Tier-A stories** — one S1, one S2, one S5 (tourism). If fewer
than three Tier-A stories exist at day 90, say so plainly in the pilot report and name
what blocked it (unrated outcomes, no recorder, unreachable contacts). That is an honest
result; a padded story list is not.

## Withdrawal and disputes

- Any organisation may withdraw a quotation, a name or the whole story at any time;
  removal is immediate and requires no justification.
- If a story misrepresents the record, the fix is to the story, never to the record.
- If someone wants to claim a result the record cannot support, the answer is a Tier B
  statement: *"this decision is still in progress"*. That sentence is allowed to be the
  honest ending of a conversation with a funder.
